The emergence of multimodal large language models and Agentic AI is transforming how information is generated, interpreted, and acted upon across the construction industry. Despite rapidly growing interest, the development trajectory, capabilities, and autonomy level of Agentic AI systems in construction remain poorly understood. This study reviews 198 publications and proposes a three-dimensional taxonomy based on agency, agentic capability, and environment interaction. The results show that 61% of studies remain within Passive AI and Augmented AI paradigms, primarily supporting prompting, retrieval, and decision-making tasks, while only 16% exhibit characteristics of Autonomous AI. Reasoning emerged as the dominant capability, whereas perception, coordination, and physical-world execution remain limited. Furthermore, 57% of studies showed no interaction with external environments, while only 22%, 8%, and 6% interacted with digital, multimodal, and physical environments, respectively. The proposed taxonomy provides a foundation for evaluating maturity and guiding future development toward more autonomous and environment-aware construction intelligence.
